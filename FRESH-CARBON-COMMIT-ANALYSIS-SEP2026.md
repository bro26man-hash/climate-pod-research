## Repository 3: DAC Materials (Computational Screening)
**Repos:** [tjz21/DAC_peroxovanadates](https://github.com/tjz21/DAC_peroxovanadates) & [tjz21/DAC_peroxotitanates](https://github.com/tjz21/DAC_peroxotitanates)  
**Stars:** 2 each  |  **Language:** Python/Materials Science  |  **Status:** 🟢 CC0 public domain

### Why It Matters
These two repositories represent the biggest open-science story in carbon capture right now: **CC0 public domain dedication**. The researcher (tjz21) has computed screening data for DAC sorbent materials — peroxovanadates and peroxotitanates — and dedicated both repos to the public domain under CC0. This is not just "open source" — it's "open everything. No rights reserved. Take it, use it, build on it."

### What the Data Represents
- **Peroxovanadates** and **peroxotitanates** are candidate sorbent materials for direct air capture
- The computational screening data tells researchers which materials might bind CO2 effectively
- By going CC0, the researcher is treating this data as public infrastructure — not intellectual property

### The CC0 Signal
This is the most significant open-science development in the carbon capture space on GitHub:
- **No other DAC repo uses CC0** — most use MIT, Apache, or no license at all
- **The data is computational screening** — not physical experiments, but the computational equivalent
- **Two separate repos, same researcher** — systematic, not opportunistic
- **September 2025 dates** — this is very recent in the context of GitHub repo lifecycles

### Episode Talking Points
- "CC0 means no copyright. No patents. No 'all rights reserved.' Just data, freely given. This researcher decided that computational screening data for carbon capture should belong to everyone."
- "Two materials, two repos, one philosophy. Peroxovanadates and peroxotitanates — both screened for DAC. The researcher is building a systematic body of work, not just sharing one-off results."
- "CC0 is the nuclear option of open science. It says 'I don't just want you to use this — I want you to own this.' That's the future of climate data."

### Development Trends
1. **Carbon capture is being mapped, not just modeled** — The two largest carbon-related repos are directories and catalogs, not simulation tools. The community is more interested in "what exists?" than "how does it work?"

2. **The economics are becoming visible** — Carbon Swarm's cost learning curves and "revenue-positive" framing suggest the field is shifting from "can we do this?" to "can we afford this?"

3. **CC0 is the new open-source for climate data** — When researchers dedicate data to the public domain, they're making a statement about what climate data is for. It's not for career advancement. It's for solving the problem.

4. **AI is entering the carbon conversation** — claude-carbon (carbon-aware computing) and the AI governance PR template in open-sustainable-technology show that the carbon capture community is grappling with AI's dual role: as a climate problem (compute emissions) and as a climate solution (carbon-aware optimization).
