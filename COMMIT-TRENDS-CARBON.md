## Five Key Findings (Updated v3)

1. **Hardware projects burst then die.** OpenAir-Cyan had 6 commits in 1 day (certification, Feb 2024), then silence for 2.5 years. The mechanism is clear: a PhD student or researcher drives the project, publishes, moves on, and nobody maintains the repo. The CITATION.cff creation shows they wanted credit, but not long-term stewardship.

2. **Literature surveys are the dominant open-source mode.** Carbon_Capture_ML (56 stars) curates papers; it doesn't simulate anything. The OpenDAC paper addition (May 2024) is the field's benchmark moment. This suggests the field is still in the knowledge-integration phase, not the tool-building phase.

3. **Academic thesis projects are ghosts.** carbon-capture-and-storage (85 stars) has zero activity since May 2021. Stars does not equal maintenance. The academic incentive structure doesn't reward long-term code stewardship. The 85 stars are a monument to citation demand, not software utility.

4. **The August 2026 wave is the most significant signal in all of climate tech.** Six independent repos across four different research approaches (sorbents, membranes, soft-sensors, electro-swing, AI) updating in a 5-day window is unprecedented. This is either a shared publication event, a coordinated community challenge, or a breakthrough that triggered parallel implementation.

5. **System integration is the missing layer.** Nobody is building the "deploy DAC at scale" code. Materials science is active; system engineering is absent. The tonyzyl soft-sensor repo is the closest exception — a practical plant-control model. But it's 16 stars and one contributor.
