# 🎙️ Carbon Capture Episode — Production Notes
## Episode Title: "The List at the Center of the Universe"

---

## Research Summary

Our GitHub investigation of carbon capture open-source projects reveals a paradox: the most valuable resource is not a simulation tool or a machine learning model — it's a **directory of links** (2,552 stars). The actual simulation and research repos are dominated by solo researchers who burn out and ghost. The one radical act we found — CC0 licensing of computational screening data — suggests that the biggest bottleneck in carbon capture isn't technology. It's distribution.

**The Big Picture:** Someone, somewhere, maintains a list of 2,500+ climate tech projects. Every few weeks, they add another link. Sometimes they fix broken links. That's it. And it has 2,552 stars.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. The Directory Is the Ecosystem
- Open-Sustainable-Technology (2,552★) is the entry point for the entire carbon capture GitHub ecosystem
- ~50 new projects added per quarter across 5 active contributors
- July 2026: Community established AI content review processes
- September 2026: Someone found and fixed broken links (digital decay matters even in curated spaces)
- **The directory proves that climate tech has a discovery problem, not a technology problem**

### 2. The CC0 Radical
- tjz21's two Direct Air Capture material screening repos use CC0 (public domain)
- Computational chemistry data — potentially worth millions in IP — given away free
- This is not standard open-source (MIT/Apache). This is "we don't own this"
- **Implication:** Maybe carbon capture breakthroughs need to be commons, not trade secrets

### 3. The Ghost Problem
- CO2-Sequestration: 32 stars, zero commits since 2019. 7 years. Two commits total.
- Carbon_Capture_ML: 56 stars, dormant since May 2024. Survey "complete."
- These repos have enthusiastic initial audiences who then... leave
- **The lesson:** Stars ≠ sustained community. A well-cited paper that nobody updates is a ghost.

### 4. Solo Research Dominates
- Except for the directory (5 contributors), every carbon capture repo has exactly 1 contributor
- No corporate OSS contributions found (no Climeworks, no Carbon Engineering repos)
- No NSF/DOE funded open-source carbon capture programs
- **The gap:** Industry isn't contributing to open source. Academic researchers are, but alone.

### 5. Biomineralization and Quantum are Emerging
- Carbonize: Coral-inspired biomineralization for multi-pollutant capture (biological CO2 fixation)
- QHackathon25: VQE quantum simulation of CO2 molecules (quantum computing meets climate)
- Both are early-stage. Both are fascinating. Neither has significant adoption.
