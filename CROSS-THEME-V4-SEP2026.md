## 🔥 Top 5 Findings from Fresh Commit Data

### 1. PCMDI Just Dropped v4.2.1 — 10 Commits in One Day
The Earth System Model evaluation toolkit had 10 commits on September 4, 2026 alone. Roundoff fix, dask optimization, SVD stability. This is what institutional maturity looks like. **Not a single commit about SRM simulation** — just evaluating whether models work.

### 2. WRF's Solar Radiation Fix (May 28) Is Buried in Routine Maintenance
The commit "correction for EOT calculation for solar radiation" is the most SRM-relevant commit in the entire solar theme, and it's framed as a bug fix. The people who maintain the atmospheric model that simulates SRM effects aren't thinking about geoengineering — they're fixing radiation schemes. The infrastructure is invisible.

### 3. The CC0 License Is the Biggest Open-Science Story in Carbon Capture
On September 12, 2025, tjz21 added a CC0 public domain dedication to the DAC_peroxovanadates repository. One commit. One sentence. But it represents a philosophical shift: computational screening data should be treated as public infrastructure, not intellectual property. Paired with the companion DAC_peroxotitanates repo (also CC0), this is a pattern that could transform DAC materials research.

### 4. OpenAir-Cyan's OSHWA Certification Is a Finish Line, Not a Starting Line
Seven commits on February 12, 2024 — OSHWA certification finalized. Then 29 months of silence. The most important open-source DAC hardware project that exists is frozen. Certification proved it works. Nobody picked it up after that.

### 5. The Ocean Has Zero Geoengineering Repos
After 10+ search queries, we found nothing. The precipitation-buoyancy POD in MDTF-diagnostics (5 commits on June 19, 2026) is the closest thing to ocean intervention code that exists, and it's an evaluation tool. The ocean's voice in the code world is indirect — it speaks through atmospheric models and diagnostic tools, not through its own simulation code.
