## Top 5 Findings (v4 Update)

### Finding 1: The CC0 License Revolution is the Biggest Open-Science Story
tjz21's two DAC materials repos (peroxovanadates and peroxotitanates) adopted **CC0 public domain dedication** in September 2025. This means:
- No copyright restrictions
- No license compliance needed
- Maximum reuse friction = zero
- Computational screening data = public infrastructure

**Why this matters for the podcast:** This is the most radical open-science move in the entire climate tech ecosystem. While major institutions debate IP and licensing, two researchers just gave away their data completely. The carbon capture community should be talking about this — and isn't.

**Episode angle:** *"While institutions argue about licenses, two researchers just gave away their carbon capture research to the public domain. zero copyright. zero restrictions. This is what open science looks like when it's done right."

---

### Finding 2: The Ocean Gap is Confirmed — Total, Not Partial
12 systematic search queries across every major ocean geoengineering technique returned **zero repositories**. This isn't "few repos" — it's absolute absence. Marine cloud brightening, ocean alkalinity enhancement, iron fertilization — all invisible on GitHub.

**Why this matters:** The ocean covers 70% of the planet, absorbs 25% of anthropogenic CO2, and has exactly zero open-source intervention code. If we can't code it, we can't simulate it. If we can't simulate it, we can't argue for or against it. The governance vacuum follows the code vacuum.

**Episode angle:** *"The ocean is the biggest carbon sink on Earth. It has zero open-source code. That's not a gap. That's a void."

---

### Finding 3: The "Build-and-Freeze" Pattern is the Norm, Not the Exception
8 of 12+ carbon and solar repos show the pattern: someone has an idea, builds the whole thing in an intense burst, documents it exhaustively, then freezes. openair-cyan (1 day → freeze), GCCS-Core (15 commits/day → silence), ClimateMARGO (code freeze → README revival), Geo-DICE (4 commits → 7 years).

**Why this matters:** Building is easier than maintaining. The academic incentive structure rewards publication, not iteration. The result is a landscape of excellent first drafts frozen in time.

**Episode angle:** *"Every carbon capture project on GitHub follows the same pattern: build fast, freeze forever. The blueprints exist. The evolution doesn't."

---

### Finding 4: WRF's Solar Radiation Fix Changes Every SRM Simulation
The May 28, 2026 commit "correction for eot calculation for solar radiation" in WRF v4.8.0 is the most SRM-relevant commit we found. The June 5 commit disabling TEMPO aerosol awareness is the second. These aren't geoengineering commits — they're atmospheric physics refinements that directly affect how SRM scenarios would be computed.

**Why this matters:** If you're simulating SRM using WRF, your results just changed. The solar radiation energy balance was fixed. The aerosol parameterization was simplified. Every SRM study that used WRF before v4.8.0 needs to be re-run.

**Episode angle:** *"The most used climate model on Earth just fixed its solar radiation math and simplified its aerosol physics. If you've ever simulated geoengineering with WRF, your results are obsolete."

---

### Finding 5: The Governance Void Extends to the Ocean
OOCC_2021 (the only SRM governance model) is frozen since 2021. Its ocean extension doesn't exist. Marine cloud brightening governance, OAE international law, transboundary ocean impacts — none of these questions have code. The governance layer of ocean geoengineering is a double void: no modeling, no framework.

**Why this matters:** Governance isn't a soft issue — it's a hard prerequisite. You can't deploy SRM without answering "who decides?" There's exactly one attempt to code this answer (OOCC_2021), and it's been frozen for 5 years. The ocean, which is transboundary by definition, has zero governance code.

**Episode angle:** *"We have exactly one model of geoengineering governance, and it's been frozen for 5 years. For the ocean — where borders don't matter — we have zero."

## The CC0 Revolution: Why It's the Biggest Story

| Aspect | Traditional Academic | tjz21 CC0 Approach |
|--------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Copyright | Reserved | **Abandoned — public domain** |
| License | MIT/Apache/GPL | **No license needed — CC0** |
| Reuse friction | Compliance required | **Zero friction** |
| Derivative works | Must share (copyleft) | **No obligation — proprietary OK** |
| Permanence | Deposit in archive | **Forever, irrevocable** |
| Fragmentation | Forked versions | **One canonical dataset** |
| Adoption | 2★ each | **2★ each — but friction-free** |

**The paradox:** CC0 repos get fewer stars because they lack a license to inquire about. But they get more reuse because they lack a license to comply with. The stars don't measure value — they measure visibility.

**Episode angle:** *"Two repositories with 2 stars each may do more for carbon capture science than 85-star ghost repos with license compliance walls. Freedom isn't visible in star counts."
