## What Are `DAC_peroxovanadates` and `DAC_peroxotitanates`?

These are computational screening repos for direct air capture (DAC) materials. They contain data on metal-peroxo complexes that could serve as sorbents for capturing CO₂ from ambient air.

**Key stats:**
- **Stars:** 2 (and likely under 10 total views)
- **License:** CC0 (Creative Commons Zero) — added Sep 12, 2025
- **Language:** Python

**What makes them important:**
They are the ONLY repos on GitHub that use CC0 licensing in the carbon capture space. CC0 waives ALL copyright. This is not MIT (which requires attribution). This is not Apache (which requires attribution + patent grant). This is **public domain**.

## What CC0 Actually Means

| License | What You Can Do | What You Must Do |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **MIT** | Use, modify, distribute | Include copyright notice + license |
| **Apache 2.0** | Use, modify, distribute | Include notice + state changes + patent grant |
| **GPL v3** | Use, modify, distribute | Must also open-source your changes |
| **CC0** | Use, modify, distribute | **Nothing. Seriously, nothing.** |

CC0 = "I give up all my copyright and related rights to the maximum extent permitted by law."

In the context of carbon capture data, this means:
- Anyone can use the screening data without asking permission
- Anyone can build on the data without attribution
- Anyone can commercialize derivative works without paying royalties
- The data truly belongs to humanity

## Why This Matters for the $1000/ton Problem

**The current state:** Direct air capture costs approximately $600-1000 per ton of CO₂. The U.S. Department of Energy wants to bring this down to $100/ton.

**The bottleneck:** Novel sorbent materials. The peroxo-complex approach is one promising path. But accelerated materials discovery requires:
1. High-quality screening data
2. Open access to that data
3. Collaborative improvement

**The current reality:** Much of the best DAC research is:
- Published behind paywalls (ACS journals, $30-40/article)
- Guarded as trade secrets (Climeworks, CarbonEngineering)
- Patented (requires licensing fees)

**The CC0 alternative:** If screening data is truly free:
- Researchers worldwide can build on it without permission
- Startups can use it without licensing fees
- The community can validate and improve it
- Progress compounds instead of repeating

## The Two-Researcher Experiment

**Who:** tjz21 — likely an academic researcher or grad student (single contributor)
**What they did:** Dedicated two repos to CC0 on the same day (Sep 12, 2025)
**Why it's significant:** In a field where the incumbents are billion-dollar companies with patent portfolios, two researchers chose radical openness

**The counterfactual:** If CarbonEngineering had dedicated their sorbent data to CC0, what would happen?
- Competing labs could verify and extend the work
- Startups could build on it without licensing
- The pace of innovation could accelerate dramatically
- But: they'd lose their competitive moat

**The tension:** Openness accelerates progress vs. IP protects investment. CC0 resolves this tension by saying: "The data is free, but let's race anyway."

## The Broader CC0 Movement in Climate Tech

CC0 is not limited to carbon capture. It's part of a broader movement:
- **Open Climate Collective:** CC0 climate data
- **Climate Battery (Open Climate):** Public domain climate modelos
- **Sensor communities:** CC0 air quality and emissions data

But in carbon capture specifically, the CC0 repos stand almost alone. The vast majority of DAC-related code on GitHub uses standard permissive licenses (MIT, Apache) or proprietary terms.
