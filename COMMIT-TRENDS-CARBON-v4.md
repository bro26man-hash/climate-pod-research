## The CC0 Revolution: A Deep Dive

The two tjz21 repositories (DAC_peroxovanadates and DAC_peroxotitanates) adopted **CC0 public domain dedication** — the strongest possible open-science commitment. Here's why this matters:

| Aspect | Traditional Academic Code | tjz21 CC0 Approach |
|--------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| License | Copyright reserved, MIT/Apache | **No rights reserved — public domain** |
| Reuse | Requires license compliance | **Zero friction — use however you want** |
| Modifications | Must share changes (copyleft) | **No obligation — proprietary derivatives OK** |
| Citation | Must cite original | **Must cite original (ethical norm) |
| Fragmentation | Forked, restricted versions | **One canonical public dataset** |

**Why CC0 for DAC materials?** Because the goal isn't to own the science — it's to accelerate it. Peroxo-vanadium and peroxo-titanium sorbents are candidates for next-gen DACC. By releasing computational screening data under CC0, the researchers ensure that no one can patent or restrict the fundamental data. The science belongs to everyone.

**Episode hook:** *"Two repositories just gave away their carbon capture research to the public domain. No copyright. No licenses. No restrictions. Just data, belonging to everyone, forever. This is what open science looks like when it's done right."*
