## Tier 2: Hardware (Frozen but Important)

### 2. openair-collective/openair-cyan ⭐76
- **Language:** Python/OpenSCAD/Electronics
- **Focus:** DIY open-source direct air capture device (OSHWA-certified)
- **Carbon Capture Relevance:** Direct — working DACC hardware blueprint
- **Last activity:** February 12, 2024 (1-day creation blitz then freeze)
- **Why it matters:** The most complete open-source DACC device design we found. OSHWA-certified means it meets open hardware standards. The blueprints, schematics, and assembly instructions are all public. But it froze after one day.
- **Status:** CONCRETE — built and documented, but no iteration
- **Podcast angle:** *"A functioning open-source carbon capture machine exists. The blueprints are public. And then... silence. Who was going to build version 2?"*

### 3. openair-collective/openair-sorbent-tester ⭐3 (NEW in v4)
- **Language:** Python/OpenSCAD
- **Focus:** Small-scale open hardware sorbent tester for moisture swing DACC materials
- **Carbon Capture Relevance:** Direct — laboratory QC tool for sorbent evaluation
- **Last activity:** January 10, 2026 (may still be ticking)
- **Why it matters:** This is the missing lab tool. You can't compare sorbent materials without a standardized tester. This device fills that gap — but with 3 stars and 1 person maintaining it, it's fragile.
- **Status:** LAB APPARATUS — purpose-built, low adoption
- **Podcast angle:** *"Before you can improve a carbon capture material, you need to test it. This open-source tester was built to do that. Only 3 people care. Is that enough?"*

## Episode Talking Points (Carbon Theme)

1. **"The hardware that froze"** — openair-cyan (76★) and openair-sorbent-tester (3★) are the only open-source DACC hardware we found. Both froze after creation. The blueprints exist. The community doesn't.

2. **"The CC0 liberation"** — tjz21's two DAC materials repos are public domain. No copyright. No restrictions. The most radical open-science move in carbon capture. Will it accelerate discovery or dissolve into obscurity?

3. **"The citation ghosts"** — 85★ and 32★ repos that haven't been touched in years. Stars measure citations, not usability. These are digital monuments, not software.

4. **"The one-day wonders"** — GCCS-Core: 15 commits in one day, then silence. A full-stack climate control framework scaffolded in 24 hours. The ambition is real. The follow-through isn't.

5. **"The continuous curator"** — The 2,552★ open-sustainable-technology directory is the only carbon capture-adjacent repo that never stops receiving updates. Infrastructure needs trustees, not just creators.
