## What is CC0?
Creative Commons Zero (CC0) is the most permissive license. It dedicates work to the public domain — removing all copyright, all restrictions. Anyone can use, modify, distribute, and commercialize without attribution.

In academic publishing, CC0 is **exceedingly rare**. Most researchers transfer copyright to publishers. CC0 says: "I don't just want you to use this. I don't want any claim on this at all."

## Why CC0 Matters for the Episode

### The Argument FOR
1. **Crisis justifies openness** — If CCS is necessary, the knowledge enabling it is a public good
2. **Accelerates iteration** — When anyone can build on your data, progress compounds
3. **Prevents weaponization** — Climate tech shouldn't be proprietary
4. **Aligns with open-source ethos** — Climate movement values align with public domain

### The Argument AGAINST
1. **No maintenance mechanism** — CC0 doesn't ensure anyone updates the data
2. **No attribution** — Researchers lose credit for labor-intensive dataset creation
3. **Discourages investment** — Companies may avoid supporting CC0 projects
4. **Quality control** — No legal framework for dispute resolution

## CC0 Precedents in Other Fields

| Field | CC0 Usage | Outcome |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Genomics | Common (genBank) | Massive acceleration of discovery |
| Weather data | Standard (NOAA, ECMWF) | Enables global forecasting |
| Wikipedia | CC0 by default | Largest knowledge base in history |
| Climate modeling | Rare | Data silos persist |
| Carbon capture | **Emerging (2 repos, 2025)** | Too early to tell |

**Lesson:** CC0 works when the community is larger than any individual's ability to maintain it. Will carbon capture reach that scale?
