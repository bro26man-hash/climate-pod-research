## 🔴 The Ocean-Intervention Gap

**This is the most striking finding:** the open-source climate-tech GitHub ecosystem has almost ZERO repositories specifically focused on ocean geoengineering. Compare:

| Theme | # Repos Found | Top Repo Stars |
|-------|--------------|----------------|
| Solar Geoengineering | 4 | 4 |
| Carbon Capture | 4 | 2 |
| Ocean Intervention | 1-2 (tangential) | 0 |

### Why the gap?
1. **Experimental complexity:** Ocean experiments require ships, permits, and massive logistics — harder to open-source than a DAC schematic or an atmospheric model.
2. **Governance sensitivity:** Ocean fertilization (e.g., LOHAWEX, SO2020) faced international legal challenges; researchers may avoid public code.
3. **Institutional siloing:** Ocean geoengineering research happens at a handful of institutions (GEOMAR Kiel, MIT, WHOI, Princeton) using internal tools.
4. **Media/political risk:** Ocean interventions are perceived as "riskier" by funders, discouraging open publication.

### What SHOULD exist:
- **Ocean alkalinity enhancement (OAE) codes:** Modeling dispersive nucleation, carbonate chemistry, alkalinity spreading
- **Iron fertilization simulators:** Phytoplankton bloom modeling, particle flux, sediment carbon sequestration
- **Seaweed/blue carbon trackers:** Farm monitoring, carbon accounting, growth modeling
- **Ocean circulation / upwelling models:** Like MITgcm or NEMO configured for geoengineering scenarios
- **Legal / governance frameworks:** International Law of the Sea compliance tools

## 🎙️ Podcast Talking Points
1. **The missing theme:** Why is ocean intervention the empty quadrant on the open-source climate-tech map?
2. **From fog to ocean:** NebuGrid as a conceptual bridge — harvesting water from the atmosphere is a miniature analog of ocean-to-land moisture transport that geoengineering aims to manipulate.
3. **Governance as code blocker:** How international regulation of ocean iron fertilization (London Convention/London Protocol) chilled open-source development.
4. **The MITgcm opportunity:** MIT's ocean circulation model is the gold standard (closed source) — an open fork for geoengineering scenarios could be transformative.
5. **Seaweed as the friendly ocean CDR:** Why seaweed farming is getting more open-source love than ocean fertilization.
6. **The legal dimension:** Open-source ocean geoengineering code could face jurisdictional barriers (high seas freedom vs. London Convention).
