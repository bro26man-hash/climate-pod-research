## 🚨 Headline Finding: The Ocean Intervention Gap

Our GitHub search across multiple query strategies returned **zero dedicated ocean geoengineering repositories.** This is the most significant finding of the entire research effort. Ocean geoengineering is the "dark matter" of climate tech on GitHub — it exists in the scientific literature but not in open code.

**See:** [ocean-intervention/README.md](https://github.com/bro26man-hash/climate-pod-research/blob/ocean-intervention/README.md)

## 📊 Commit Trend Highlights

### What the commit histories tell us:

1. **Institutional bursts dominate climate software** — PCMDI's 133★ had 10 commits in 2 days (v4.2.1 release). Most repos can't sustain this.

2. **The August 2026 DAC materials wave** — Three repositories (peroxovanadates, peroxotitanates, electro-swing DAC) were all active on Aug 19, 2026, suggesting a coordinated research event in open computational chemistry.

3. **Ocean geoengineering is silent** — No ocean intervention repositories show meaningful activity. The absence itself is a governance and institutional story.

4. **Legacy Fortran dominates** — AM3 (2015) and PMIP analyzers are Fortran-based. The modern Python/data-science shift hasn't reached geoengineering-specific simulation.

5. **Dormancy is the default** — 10 of 14 discovered repos show no activity in the last year. Climate tech open source is fragile.

**See:** [COMMIT-TRENDS.md](https://github.com/bro26man-hash/climate-pod-research/blob/main/COMMIT-TRENDS.md)
