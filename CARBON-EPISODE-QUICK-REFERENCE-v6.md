## Talking Points

### Opening Hook
> "Two researchers put their direct air capture data in the public domain — no patents, no trade secrets, no licensing. Just: it's yours. While Climeworks guards its formulas like nuclear codes, these scientists chose the opposite. This is the CC0 revolution in carbon capture."

### The CC0 Revolution
- Sep 12, 2025: Two DAC materials repos (peroxovanadates + peroxotitanates) both adopted CC0
- CC0 waives ALL copyright — more permissive than MIT, Apache, or GPL
- In climate tech, where IP is hoarded by billion-dollar companies, CC0 is revolutionary
- Removes the "data moat" — the competitive advantage of being first to discover
- Episode question: Can open-source collaboration accelerate DAC materials discovery?

### The OSHWA Blitz
- OpenAir-Cyan: 6 commits in 1 day (Feb 12, 2024) → OSHWA certification
- Then: 2.6 years of complete silence
- CI/CD workflows deleted 3 months BEFORE the blitz — no automated testing infrastructure
- Got the sticker without the infrastructure
- Episode question: Can certification substitute for maintenance?

### The AI Governance Signal
- Open-Sustainable-Tech directory introduced AI content review PR template (Jul 2026)
- 2,552 projects, now requiring human review for AI-generated entries
- Open-source ecosystem fighting AI pollution
- Episode angle: "The directory is so big that AI is trying to fool it."

### The Frozen Survey
- Carbon_Capture_ML: structured survey of ML for carbon capture
- Built Jan-Feb 2023, final commit May 2024
- 2.5 years of silence in a field publishing weekly
- Episode angle: "A stale survey is worse than no survey — it shows you a map of a territory that's already changed."

### Closing Question
> "Can open source break the $1000/ton DAC cost barrier? The CC0 researchers think so. The OSHWA blitz looks like it tried but stopped. The frozen survey proves the field is moving faster than its documentation. What does it all add up to?"
