## The Five Cutting-Edge Signals (v5 Additions)

### 1. 🔬 The Weitzman Discounting Revolution (Solar)
srm-forever's Aug 26 burst adopted Weitzman certainty-equivalent discounting, shifting the SRM-vs-mitigation cost comparison from expected-utility to certainty-equivalent framework. Under deep uncertainty, this **always favors more cautious, long-term actions** — potentially undermining the "SRM is cheap" argument.

**Impact on episode:** This is the single most significant intellectual development this quarter. It transforms the solar geoengineering episode from "is SRM feasible?" to "is SRM economically rational under deep uncertainty?"

### 2. 📊 The Directory Machine (Carbon)
Open-Sustainable-Tech added 5 entries in a single day (Sep 19: DigitalizationSupportHub, MicroPowerManager, GreenHEART). The directory now holds 2,500+ projects. But no individual DAC project is close to breaking the $1000/ton barrier. The directory is infrastructure, not invention.

**Impact on episode:** "The world's biggest climate-tech directory doesn't build anything — it just lists. But who decides what gets listed?"

### 3. 🏝️ The Digital Twin Signal (Carbon)
dac-moving-bed-digital-twin (Jul 2026) represents a shift from invention-first to optimization-first for DAC. Instead of trying new chemistry, simulate the existing process in detail to find optimization opportunities. A virtual replica of a physical DAC system.

**Impact on episode:** "Instead of inventing better DAC chemistry, why not just simulate the existing process better?"

### 4. 🌊 The Marine Cloud Brightening Burst (Solar/Ocean Adjacent)
MCB's Jun 16 burst (10 commits, temperature + cloud-cover analysis notebooks) is the closest thing to ocean-intervention code on GitHub. But it's technically solar geoengineering, not ocean intervention. The MCB project is the **gateway drug** — if marine cloud brightening is acceptable, does it open the door to ocean alkalinity enhancement and sea salt spray?

**Impact on episode:** "MCB is the ocean's closest friend on GitHub — but it's not really ocean intervention. It's solar geoengineering with an ocean source."

### 5. 🌑 The Persistent Ocean Vacuum (Ocean)
After 15+ search queries, the ocean geoengineering gap remains **complete and unmovable**. Zero repos. Zero code. Zero governance. The gap is structural, not accidental — five hypotheses explain it (funding, governance, complexity, community, taboo).

**Impact on episode:** "The ocean is the dark matter of climate tech on GitHub — invisible, undetected, and yet presumably massive in the scientific literature."

## Cross-Cutting Trends Across All Three Themes

### Trend A: The Burst-and-Sleep Pattern
Both srm-forever (4 commits/1 day) and MCB (10 commits/1 day) show **intense single-day commits followed by long dormancy.** This is the "poke-and-hope" model — big effort spikes, then silence. This contrasts with institutional steady-state (WRF, PCMDI) and suggests that creative breakthroughs come from individuals, not committees.

### Trend B: The Governance Emergence
The discovery of climate-intervention-governance (Zereo0317, Aug 2026) in the solar episode and its relevance to the ocean episode (London Protocol) signals that **governance is becoming a real research area** — not just a policy afterthought. Climate tech is moving from "can we do it?" to "should we, and who decides?"

### Trend C: The CC0 Slowing
In v4, we documented a "CC0 public-domain revolution" in carbon capture. In v5, **no new CC0 DAC projects appeared**. The revolution may have transformed into something quieter — the directory approach (Open-Sustainable-Tech) rather than the public-domain approach (CC0).

### Trend D: The AI Gravity Well
Across all three themes, AI/ML projects are multiplying: claude-carbon (carbon), Carbon_Capture_ML survey (carbon), Direct-Air-Capture ML (carbon). But the frozen Carbon_Capture_ML survey (2.5 years) suggests papers don't lead to deployed systems. The AI gravity well is strong but the deployment pipeline is weak.

### Trend E: The Infrastructure vs. Invention Gap (Universal)
In solar: WRF and PCMDI maintain infrastructure; srm-forever has creative bursts.
In carbon: Open-Sustainable-Tech catalogs what exists; no DAC project breaks the cost barrier.
In ocean: MDTF evaluates models; no one writes intervention code.

**The pattern is universal:** Infrastructure grows, but invention is sporadic. The institutions build the tools; the individuals have the ideas. The gap between infrastructure and invention is where the podcast stories live.
