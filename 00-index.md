## 🎙️ Episode Overview

Carbon capture spans two very different open-source regimes: **(a)** *materials science* — computational chemistry for next-generation sorbents and catalysts, and **(b)** *system engineering* — process design, cost modeling, and deployment planning. The August 2026 surge in DAC materials repositories suggests a **new wave of open computational chemistry** that could accelerate sorbent discovery beyond the $1,000/ton cost barrier.

## 🎙️ Episode Narrative Arc

1. **Hook** — The August 2026 wave of open DAC materials simulations is unprecedented. But can open code really crack the $1,000/ton barrier?
2. **The Materials Layer** — Peroxovanadates and peroxotitanates: what does it mean to simulate sorbents atom-by-atom in the open?
3. **The Hardware Layer** — Electro-swing DAC as open hardware: democratizing capture, one stack at a time.
4. **The Economics Layer** — ClimateMARGO.jl models the_policy_ problem: when does DAC make sense vs. cutting emissions?
5. **The Gap** — System engineering (OpenCarbon) is quiet while materials science surges. Why?
6. **The Path Forward** — Open-source DAC needs both: better materials *and* better system design tools.
