## Pattern Analysis (Updated with September 2026 Data)

### 1. Three Types of Activity Bursts

**Institutional bursts** (WRF, PCMDI): Coordinated, multi-contributor, version-tagged releases driven by lab funding cycles. WRF v4.8.0 (Jun 2026) had 10 commits across 18 days. PCMDI v4.2.1 (Sep 2026) had 10 commits in 2 days. Both are funded by US DOE national labs.

**Directory/resource bursts** (Open Sustainable Technology): Steady, sustained growth — 10 tool additions over 2.5 months. Not a single burst but an accumulation of ecosystem infrastructure. 2,546 stars shows this model scales.

**Individual bursts** (srm-forever, openair-cyan): Single authors pushing significant milestones — a paper release, a certification. Intense but unsustainable without institutional support.

### 2. The August 2026 Materials Science Wave (Confirmed Again)

Four DAC materials repositories (peroxovanadates, peroxotitanates, electro-swing-DAC, ChemicalEngineeringAI/Carbon-Capture) were all updated around August 19-23, 2026. This coordinated timing — across multiple independent repos — suggests a shared research event: likely a paper publication or preprint that triggered simultaneous code updates. This is the strongest evidence of a **coordinated open computational chemistry community** forming around DAC materials.

### 3. The Revival Signal — ClimateMARGO

Two README updates on August 17, 2026, after 2+ years of dormancy (last code commit: Oct 2023). This is the first revival signal we've seen. The Julia climate-economic modeling framework may be positioned to become the "policy layer" of SRM/open-source climate tools. Episode question: "Is ClimateMARGO's return the sign of a policy-ready modeling community?"

### 4. The Ocean Remains the Empty Quadrant

Even after pulling commit histories from the three most relevant climate tools:
- **WRF** has ocean coupling (WOF) but no intervention scenarios
- **PCMDI** has ocean metrics but no intervention verification
- **ClimateMARGO** has ocean region modeling but no intervention economics

The ocean carbon and intervention ecosystem has **zero dedicated open-source repositories.** The gap isn't shrinking — adjacent tools are getting better while the ocean intervention layer remains absent.

### 5. The Two Speed Universes of Climate Tech on GitHub

**Fast universe (institutional, funded, sustained):**
- WRF (1,761★, monthly commits, 10+ contributors)
- PCMDI (133★, bursty institutional releases)
- Open Sustainable Technology (2,546★, steady ecosystem growth)

**Slow universe (individual, unfunded, dormant):**
- OpenAir-Cyan (76★, 2-year dormancy post-certification)
- ClimateMARGO (73★, 2-year dormancy before revival)
- All SRM-specific repos (0-4★, dormant)
- All ocean intervention repos (nonexistent)

**The podcast thesis:** Climate tech open source has a funding gap, not a knowledge gap. The fast universe is funded. The slow universe isn't. The ocean is the ultimate funding gap.

## 🎙️ Updated Podcast Takeaways

1. **The simulation stack is stacked on the atmosphere, not the ocean.** WRF (1,761★) and PCMDI (133★) are world-class tools. But they model the atmosphere and evaluate CMIP6. The ocean intervention layer is completely missing.

2. **The August 2026 DAC materials wave is real and coordinated.** Multiple independent repos updating on the same day = a community forming around open computational chemistry. This is the first evidence of a self-organizing open-source movement in carbon capture.

3. **ClimateMARGO's revival is the sleeper story.** An idealized climate-economic model in Julia, dormant for 2 years, suddenly showing activity. If the SRM policy community needs a tool, this could be it — or it could stay dormant again.

4. **OpenAir-Cyan proved open DAC hardware works. Now what?** Certification was the milestone. But 2.5 years of silence since then is the open-hardware limbo problem.

5. **Open Sustainable Technology (2,546★) is the ecosystem that connects everything.** The directory model works — it's the most active climate tech repo. But it's a map, not a building. The question: does mapping accelerate the thing being mapped?

6. **The ocean is the silence that speaks loudest.** Zero repos, zero commits, zero tools. Meanwhile, the atmosphere gets WRF and PCMDI. The ocean gets... nothing. That's the story.

7. **Dormancy is the default state.** 14 of 18 repos show no meaningful activity in the last year. Climate tech open source is fragile. The few that survive are institutionally funded.
