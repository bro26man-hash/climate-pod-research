## Pattern Analysis

### 1. Two Types of Activity Bursts

**Institutional bursts** (PCMDI, 10 commits/2 days): Coordinated, multi-contributor, version-tagged releases driven by lab funding cycles.

**Individual bursts** (srm-forever, 4 commits/1 day; openair-cyan, 6 commits/1 day): Single authors pushing significant milestones — a paper release, a certification, a tool launch.

### 2. The August 2026 Materials Science Wave

Three DAC materials repositories (peroxovanadates, peroxotitanates, electro-swing-DAC) were all active on **August 19, 2026**. This coordinated timing suggests a shared research event — likely a paper publication or conference presentation that triggered simultaneous code/publication updates. This is the first evidence of a **coordinated open computational chemistry community** forming around DAC materials.

### 3. Ocean is the Empty Quadrant

No ocean geoengineering repository showed meaningful commit activity. The closest ocean-adjacent project (NebuGrid, fog-harvesting) had a single update in August 2026. Everything else in the ocean intervention space is absent from GitHub.

### 4. Legacy Fortran Dominates What Exists

AM3 (2015) and PMIP analyzers are Fortran-based. The modern Python/data-science shift (PCMDI, xCDAT) hasn't reached the geoengineering-specific simulation space. This is a story about institutional inertia in climate software.

### 5. Dormancy is the Default

Most repos are dormant: OpenCarbon (3 years), CarbonNeg (10 months), Arctic Impacts (2 years), Earth Digital Twin (11 months). Only PCMDI and OpenAir-Cyan show sustained activity. **Climate tech open source is fragile** — most projects die after an initial burst.
