## Act 1: The Map-Makers (12 min)

### Opening Image
> "There are 2,552 people watching a directory. Not a model. Not a device. A directory. It contains 2,500+ projects — but how many of them are actually about capturing carbon? And who's adding new entries? Five contributors are pushing 5 new projects per week. And one of them just modified the PR template to handle AI-generated content. That's not a directory anymore. That's an ecosystem."

### Open-Sustainable-Technology
- **2,552★** — the largest climate tech repo on GitHub
- 5 active contributors, 2,500+ projects
- 5 new projects added per week
- AI content review PR template (July 19, 2026) — governance adaptation
- **Key question:** What percentage of 2,500+ projects are actually CCUS?

### Carbon_Capture_ML
- **56★** — the paper tracker
- Curated survey of ML papers for carbon capture
- Anchored by OpenDAC (May 2024) — the benchmark
- **Key question:** After the survey, what's next?

### Act 1 Key Insight
> "The carbon capture software ecosystem has a directory problem. We can catalog 2,500+ projects, but we can't answer a simple question: 'What's the shortest path from sorbent discovery to commercial deployment?' The directory is the map, but there's no compass."

## Act 2: The Garage Builders (15 min)

### OpenAir-Cyan — The OSHWA-Certified DACC Device
- **76★** — high for hardware, highest for CCUS hardware
- OSHWA UID: US001095
- 7 commits on February 12, 2024 — all about certification, zero code
- 20 months before and 20 months after: silence
- **Key question:** Is this a working device or a concept?

### The CC0 Pair — TJ Z21's Radical Openness
- **2★ each** — practically invisible
- DAC_peroxovanadates + DAC_peroxotitanates
- CC0 public domain license
- Synchronized updates on September 23, 2025
- **Key question:** Why CC0? Because IP is part of the problem.

### The Ghost — Carbon Capture and Storage
- **85★** — highly cited, zero maintenance
- Dormant since March 2021
- MATLAB-only — no community contributions possible
- **Key question:** What happens when a repo becomes a citation monument?

### Act 2 Key Insight
> "The carbon capture open-source ecosystem has three archetypes: the garage builder who certified a DIY device and then went silent, the data donor who gave away the research to the public domain, and the ghost that 85 papers cite but nobody runs."

---

## Act 3: The Missing Layer (15 min)

### What's Missing?
1. **No full-system DAC simulation** — we have materials screening, but not system-level modeling
2. **No economic modeling** — srm-forever covers solar geoengineering costs, not DAC costs
3. **No continuous innovation loop** — OpenAir-Cyan burst and froze
4. **No license standardization** — Open-Sustainable-Tech doesn't enforce licenses
5. **No experimental validation pipeline** — computational screening needs a path to lab testing

### What Would Break the $1000/Ton Barrier?
1. **Sorbent innovation** via computational screening (TJ Z21's approach, scaled up)
2. **Process optimization** via ML (Carbon_Capture_ML's survey, implemented)
3. **Modular hardware** via DIY (OpenAir-Cyan's approach, scaled to manufacturing)
4. **Public domain data** via CC0 (TJ Z21's approach, proliferated)
5. **Community coordination** via better directories (Open-Sustainable-Tech, synthesized)

### The Structural Problem
- **Materials scientists** screen sorbents (TJ Z21) but don't build devices (OpenAir-Cyan)
- **Device builders** certify hardware (OpenAir-Cyan) but don't model economics (gap)
- **Directory keepers** catalog projects (Open-Sustainable-Tech) but don't synthesize (gap)
- **Paper trackers** index literature (Carbon_Capture_ML) but don't validate (gap)
- **Ghost repositories** provide methods (carbon-capture-and-storage) but nobody maintains them (gap)

### Act 3 Key Insight
> "Every piece of the $1000/ton puzzle exists in open source. Sorbent screening. Device certification. Literature surveys. Economic frameworks. But they exist in separate silos. TJ Z21 doesn't talk to OpenAir-Cyan. Carbon_Capture_ML doesn't talk to carbon-capture-and-storage. The breakthrough won't come from any one repo — it'll come from connecting them."

## Closing Segment (8 min)

### The OSHWA Moment
> "On February 12, 2024, K Collins pushed 7 commits to GitHub. No code. Just certification paperwork. But that paperwork — OSHWA UID US001095 — made OpenAir-Cyan the first certified open-source carbon capture device in history. You can 3D-print it. You can build it. You can modify it. The company trying to do this commercially charges $500 a ton."

### The CC0 Declaration
> "TJ Z21 looked at the perovskite screening data, the peroxovanadate results, the peroxotitanate results, and said: 'This is everyone's.' CC0. No patents. No licensing. No 'contact us for collaboration.' Just data, freely available."

### The Ghost Warning
> "85 stars. Zero commits since 2021. carbon-capture-and-storage is a monument to academic impact. Papers cite it, but nobody runs it. The MATLAB dependency is the cage."

### Call to Action
- **Materials scientists:** TJ Z21's CC0 data is waiting for your experimental validation
- **Hardware hackers:** OpenAir-Cyan needs builders, not just certifiers
- **ML engineers:** Carbon_Capture_ML's OpenDAC benchmark needs implementations
- **Python developers:** carbon-capture-and-storage is waiting for its port
- **Everyone:** Check Open-Sustainable-Tech — add your CCUS project to the directory

### Final Quote
> "The $1000/ton barrier isn't made of titanium and amines. It's made of coordination failure. We have the sorbents. We have the devices. We have the data. We have the surveys. What we don't have is the connective tissue."
